## What this is, and why it comes first

It is a **peg and a row of holes**. The holes are all meant to be the same size as
the peg, except each one is cut very slightly bigger than the last — six of them,
each one five hundredths of a millimetre roomier than its neighbour.

Here is why that is worth a print. The cartridge-clasp plate asks you to rank four
lids that differ by **eight hundredths of a millimetre**. That is thinner than a
sheet of paper. If your printer's own error is bigger than that, then the four lids
are not really different from each other — you would be ranking the printer's mood,
not the design, and you would get a confident answer that meant nothing.

**So: before I ask you to judge eight hundredths of a millimetre, I would like to
know whether this machine can hold it.** The peg and the holes tell us that, and
they tell us using your hands. No measuring tools needed.

There are also **three plain blocks**. Those are for the kitchen scale.

changed halfway.

## What to do with the parts

**The row of holes.** One end of the block has a **cut-off corner**. That end is
hole number 1. Count from there.

Take the peg and try it in each hole in turn, **1 to 6**, and for each one tell me
which of these it was:

- **won't go** — it simply does not start
- **tight** — it goes, but you are pushing harder than feels comfortable
- **snug** — it goes with a firm, deliberate push and holds when you let go
- **easy** — it slides in and stays put
- **loose** — it drops in, and it rattles or falls out if you tip the block

There is no right answer and no wrong answer. **I fully expect hole 1 to refuse the
peg** — hole 1 is cut to exactly the same size as the peg, and two things the same
size do not fit into each other. If hole 1 takes the peg easily, that is a real and
useful result, not a mistake: it means the machine prints holes wider than asked.

Use the **same peg** for all six holes and keep the second one clean as a spare.

**The three blocks.** Weigh each one on the kitchen scale, separately, and write
down all three numbers — even if they look identical. Especially if they look
identical. The scale's resolution is worth recording too.

## What this cannot tell us

It is one plate, printed once, in one orientation, in one filament. It is **not** a
measurement of your printer's accuracy, not a tolerance, not a qualification of
anything, and it says nothing at all about whether the cartridge design works. It
answers one narrow question — *can this machine hold eight hundredths?* — and that
question has to be answered before the clasp plate is worth your time.

**Nothing here is a safety test and nothing here needs buying.** If the print fails
or the parts come out obviously wrong, that is also an answer; send a photo and stop.
