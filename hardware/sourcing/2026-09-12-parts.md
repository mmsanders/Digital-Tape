## 2. The 4 GB V30 requirement

Michael's direction is recorded: **no 64 GB cards; prefer cheap 4 GB V30; micro or full-size.**
This round searched for that combination. **It was not found, and two independent reasons
suggest it does not exist as a product rather than merely being out of stock.**

### 2.1 Reason one — the marking

Video Speed Class (V6…V90) is a marking aimed at sustained video capture, and it is applied to
the high-capacity consumer cards that market targets. Every V30 part located in this search was
**32 GB or larger**; no source located described a V30-marked part at 4 GB. Nothing in the SD
specification obviously *forbids* a 4 GB SDHC card carrying V30 — a 4 GB card is SDHC, and
Video Speed Class is defined for SDHC — so this is a **commercial** absence, not a
demonstrated technical impossibility. `sdcard.org` is blocked from here, so **this paragraph
rests on secondary sources and not on the SD Association's own text.**

### 2.2 Reason two — what 4 GB actually is now

The consumer 4 GB card has left distribution. What remains at 4 GB is **industrial SLC**, and
it is priced accordingly. DigiKey's entire 4 GB microSD offering on 12 September 2026:

Source: DigiKey catalogue, 12 September 2026. DigiKey stocks **no SanDisk microSD at all**

**So "cheap 4 GB" and "4 GB V30" fail together.** The cheapest 4 GB part available to me is
~$168 and carries no V30 marking; a 32 GB consumer V30 card is roughly an order of magnitude

## 2.6 Price tiers — Michael's under-$20 / under-$30 question

Asked directly on 12 September: the cheapest cards that satisfy the requirement, as close to
disposable as possible; anything under $20, or under $30?

**Why "disposable" is the right instinct and not just thrift.** ADR-105 makes the microSD
**captive inside every cartridge**. So the card price is a **per-cartridge BOM cost**, paid
again for every cartridge the family ever owns — not a one-off bench expense. That is what
makes the $167.81 industrial part impossible as a product part rather than merely expensive,
and it is the strongest argument in this document for a consumer-priced card.

with a date. Consumer retail is blocked, so no SanDisk/Samsung/Lexar price can be confirmed here.

**This is a decision, not a substitution, and it is not mine to make.** U3 (UHS Speed Class 3,
SD 3.01) and V30 (Video Speed Class 30, SD 5.0) both nominally denote a **30 MB/s minimum
sustained write** — so on the number that WP-05 actually cares about they state the same floor.
But they are **different marks under different parts of the specification**, and a card marked
U3 is not thereby marked V30. CLAUDE.md §5 forbids silently substituting a weaker speed class,
and quietly treating U3 as V30 is exactly that substitution even if it turns out to be
harmless. **PM/Michael decide whether the U3 mark satisfies the requirement.** If it does, the
32 GB card at $6.80 and the 16 GB at $5.70 change the whole economics.

  $5.70, both with a wider temperature range than the V30 parts.

### Two risks that come with the cheap parts

1. **Brand and revision stability.** HTsemi is not a major-brand card, and WP-05 binds every
   atomicity result to an exact SKU, revision and CID precisely because a vendor can change
   controller or NAND between production runs without changing the part number. On an obscure
   part that risk is higher, not lower, and **a qualification that passes on one lot may not
   describe the next one.** The stock figures (~1,000,000 on every line) also look like
   placeholder quantities rather than real counts; treat them as "available", not as a number.
2. **Temperature.** The V30 parts are 0…70 °C. `spec/hw/cartridge-shell.md` §11 (SH-4) already
   flags a cartridge left on a car dashboard, and 0 °C is not a generous cold limit for a toy
   that goes outside. The cheaper U3 parts are **−25…85 °C**, which is better on exactly the
   axis a child's device needs. That is an argument for the U3 family beyond price.

**No purchase is proposed and no capacity or speed class is substituted.** Two decisions come
first: whether the U3 mark satisfies the V30 requirement, and which capacity the six-SKU set is
drawn from.

**An anomaly worth recording.** DigiKey's own listing for NXP `74HC221DB112` shows a supply
range of **4.5–5.5 V** — an HC part number in an HCT-shaped envelope. Whether that is a
catalogue data error or a real variant, **selecting it would put the one-shot outside its
supply range at this rail**, and nothing in the analysis would have noticed. That anomaly is
now a gate: `check_criteria()` in `hardware/thermal/solenoid_timing.py` fails if the bound
part's supply envelope does not contain `V_LOGIC`, and the check has a negative control
demonstrating it goes red.

**What binding the part does not do.** It does not close IR-018-16. The supply envelope is
sourced; the **guaranteed pulse-width limits at 3.3 V are not**, because every host serving
that table is blocked. `TIMING_BOUND_VERIFIED` stays `False`, the §6 verdict stays
**PROVISIONAL**, and `make -C hardware fabrication-gate` stays **CLOSED**.
