# WP-24 — Cartridge shell and carrier PCB

**Stream:** hardware · **Owner:** Hardware Lead designs, Michael prints and ranks
**Status:** in flight — clasp assessment delivered, variants on the WP04-01 plate
**Depends on:** nothing blocking · **Blocks:** WP-23 (enclosure), WP-25 (abuse testing)

## Interface

A two-part printed shell holding a captive microSD on a carrier PCB (ADR-105). It presents
edge contacts to the player's slot (`spec/hw/board-rev-a.md` §8) and nothing else. No screw, no
battery, no electronics beyond the carrier.

**The shell's contract with the rest of the project is short:** it keeps a microSD captive
against a child and a drop, it opens for a parent with a tool, and it does not look like a
memory card in a costume.

Full assessment: **`spec/hw/cartridge-shell.md`**. Analysis `hardware/mech/clasp.py`, geometry
`hardware/cad/cartridge/shell.py`, checks `hardware/cad/cartridge/test_shell.py`.

## The decision this package rests on

Michael chose a **clasp, not a screw**, and a near-invisible seam (issue #6, Decisions 006 §2).
The PM's reframing is what makes it feasible and it is worth restating because it inverts the
problem: **a cartridge is opened one to three times in its life**, not weekly, because every
cartridge after the first is loaded by the device's own copy button. A snap that must survive
thousands of cycles is a fatigue problem no printed material solves. A snap that must survive
five is an interference fit.

That is also why the material correction in Decisions 006 §1 costs this package nothing:
nylon's advantage is fatigue life in a small flexure, and this flexure does not need any.

## Acceptance criteria

From `spec/hw/cartridge-shell.md` §1. **S-1 is the PM's proposal, accepted. S-2 to S-4 are
added, and they are the ones that can fail** — S-1 cannot, because the design holds zero strain
at rest.

| # | Criterion | Where it runs |
|---|---|---|
| S-1 | 20 open-close cycles, retention force within 20 % of first cycle | WP-24, in PETG |
| S-2 | 90 days closed at 23 °C **and** at 45 °C, then S-1's force within 20 % | WP-24, long lead — starts when the first PETG pair exists |
| S-3 | 1.0 m drop, closed, six faces and four corners, card retained every time | **WP-25**, with the rest of abuse testing |
| S-4 | **A 30 N pinch held 10 s does not open it**, no tool | WP-24. Safety-critical |
| S-5 | Blind ranking of the interference sweep, lids not confounding | WP04-01, this plate |

**S-4 is written to the Decisions 006 §5 standard from the start** — witnessed by Michael,
independently audited by the Verification Lead from the method and the raw data, not from being
present. Record it with `hardware/measurements/TEMPLATE.md`. A 30 N pinch was chosen partly
because a $10 luggage scale can read it, which is what makes the raw data auditable at all.

## What is on the plate

WP04-01 rev 4 carries four bases at 0.15 / 0.25 / 0.35 / 0.45 mm interference, blind-lettered,
plus **two identical lids as a control**: if the ranking tracks which lid was used rather than
which base, the shared part is wearing and the sweep is measuring that instead (the same trick
as WP-04's `D`/`H` bed controls, aimed at a different confound).

`tpu-lip.stl` ships beside the plate and **cannot be printed at the library** — Shasta runs PLA
only, and a merged STL carries one material for every part in it.

Every branch from the result is decided in advance in `spec/hw/cartridge-shell.md` §8, so the
next plate goes out without another round of thinking.

## The sealed alternative, settled

Michael's option 2 — seal the shell permanently, load over USB-C — is assessed in
`spec/hw/cartridge-shell.md` §10. **Recommended against**, agreeing with the PM, and the
trigger they set for reconsidering (*"if a printed clasp cannot hold a card safely"*) is not
met. USB-MSC is sized at **6 weeks ± 2** as a Phase 5 enhancement that needs no board change
and no shell change — which is itself an argument for the clasp, because the clasp does not
close that door.

**That estimate is an outside one and the Software Lead should correct it.** Firmware is not
this stream, and a number for someone else's work should be checked rather than inherited.
